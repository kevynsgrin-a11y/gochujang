Gochujang Shakshuka

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 25 minutes · Total: About 35 minutes · Serves 3–4 (one pan)

Ingredients

The base

  • 2 tbsp olive oil (about 25 g)
  • 1 onion (about 150 g), sliced; 2 bell peppers, red (about 250 g), sliced
  • 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g), sliced
  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru or hot paprika (about 3 g); 1 tsp ground cumin (about 2.5 g); a pinch of allspice (about 1 g)
  • 800 g canned crushed tomatoes
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml) — the umami bridge, standing where salt would
  • 1 tsp honey or sugar (about 5 g), if the tomatoes run tart

The eggs and finish

  • 5 eggs (about 250 g)
  • 100 g feta, crumbled; a handful of cilantro or parsley (about 10 g); pickled onion or a squeeze of lemon
  • Bread (about 300 g), warm, for the sopping

Instructions

  1. Onion and peppers in the oil at medium-high, 8–10 minutes to soft and slumped; the garlic and the paste in, fried together 45 seconds until darkened and fragrant; the gochugaru, cumin, and allspice a 30-second toast in the same pan. Checkpoint — the pan's smell at this moment is the fusion in miniature — the peppers' sweetness, the paste's ferment, the cumin's warm dust — and it should smell like one dish, not two cuisines shaking hands awkwardly.
  2. The crushed tomatoes and soy in; a steady simmer 10 minutes, until the sauce thickens enough to hold a spoon's trail and the oil separates in red-orange drops at the edges — the shakshuka doneness sign, unchanged by the substitution. Checkpoint — the sauce's taste: tomato-sweet first, a warm chile hum, the ferment's depth beneath, a sharp finish — if it reads "spicy tomato sauce" flatly, it wants the soy's salt-carrier and a minute more reduction.
  3. Five wells in the sauce, an egg into each, lid on, 4–6 minutes to set whites and molten yolks — the egg-window every cuisine respects. Checkpoint — the whites just opaque, the yolks domed and liquid; a test yolk should break into the sauce at the touch of bread. Feta, herbs, and pickled onion over the pan.
  4. The pan to the table on its trivet, bread armed. Checkpoint — the full bite — bread dragged through yolk, sauce, and feta together: the yolk's richness, the tomato's sweet acid, the paste's ferment-warmth, the feta's brine. Four flavors that met this morning, tasting like old colleagues.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The sauce base keeps 4 days and improves — the make-ahead move (eggs poached fresh in the reheated base, 4 minutes, any night of the week). Leftover base over rice or the #4 bowl is the crossover the fridge will suggest on its own. - Variations: crumbled merguez or the #13's seared pork beside the eggs; a spoon of the kimchi (#2) chopped through the base for the two-ferment pot; milder dial for brunch, fuller for dinner.
