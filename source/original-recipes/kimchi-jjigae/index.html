Kimchi Jjigae

Prep: 10 minutes · Cook: 20 minutes · Total: About 30 minutes · Serves 3–4 (one pot)

Ingredients

The star and the pot

  • 400 g sour, well-fermented baechu kimchi (#2's elder stage or any jar two-plus weeks old), cut into bite lengths, juice included
  • 200 g pork belly or shoulder, sliced 5 mm (or a can of tuna in oil — the bachelor classic, honestly labeled)

The broth

  • 400 ml water or anchovy-kelp stock
  • 1.5 tbsp gochujang (about 30 g), dissolved
  • 1 tbsp gochugaru (about 6 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce or the kimchi jar's brine (15 ml)
  • A pinch of sugar (about 3 g) — rounding, not sweetening

The rest

  • 200 g firm tofu, thick-slabbed; 1 onion (about 100 g), sliced; 2 scallions (about 30 g) and 1 green chili (about 8 g), sliced; 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated; 5 ml sesame oil, finishing; instant ramen noodles or cooked rice for the pot's final act, optional

Instructions

  1. The pork rendered in the dry pot first — 3–4 minutes, its fat becoming the pot's cooking base (the tuna path: the oil and the drained fish, 2 minutes). The kimchi in over the rendered pork and fried 2 minutes in the fat — this brief fry drives the kimchi's raw sour edge off and deepens the whole pot before any liquid joins. Checkpoint — the kimchi's edges go translucent and the pot's smell shifts from sharp-sour to round-sour-savory — the fry's whole achievement, worth the two minutes it costs.
  2. The stock, the dissolved gochujang, the gochugaru, and the soy in; the onion too. A steady bubble, 10 minutes, lid ajar — the broth turns the kimchi's red-orange and the pork's fat beads at the surface. Checkpoint — a spoon of broth at the 10-minute mark: sour first, then deep chile warmth, then the pork's richness — the three-part register that defines the dish. Flat sour? The jar ran young (see the audit). One-note hot? The gochugaru ran past its six grams.
  3. The tofu slabs laid in gently, 5 more minutes — warmed through, not boiled apart. The scallions, chili, garlic, and sesame oil over the top, and the pot goes to the table in its own vessel, still bubbling. Checkpoint — the eating test with rice: kimchi and broth over the rice bowl, a slab of tofu beside, and the pot's sour-heat should open the appetite rather than close it — the jjigae's entire purpose. The noodle or rice drop at the pot's final act (the last five minutes of broth, never longer) is the table's shared last course, and the fight over it is the meal's traditional ending.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days and genuinely improves; day-two jjigae over fresh rice is the dish's own finest hour. (The dolsot's components (#4) and this pot share a fridge rotation.) - Variations: the tuna pot (the honest bachelor classic); the ramen drop; spam's island fork; the brothier soup version with more stock and no reduction for the summer table.
