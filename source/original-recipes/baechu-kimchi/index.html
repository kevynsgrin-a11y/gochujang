**Lane: modern — the paste-loud build, and the honest confession that comes with it.** Straight talk, because this site runs on it: traditional kimchi does not use gochujang. The pot's chile arrives as gochugaru flakes, and any jar telling you otherwise is selling shortcuts. But this site's whole argument is what the paste can do, and in kimchi it does something real — a spoonful in the paste adds the ferment's roundness and a deeper red, a modern build the home fermenters of the last twenty years arrived at honestly. This record makes that jar, labels it plainly, and runs a faster ferment than the traditional batch (kbbqguide#B01's slow 1–3-day room stage) — warmer, shorter, punchier. **Heat dial: 3.**

Baechu Kimchi

Prep: 1 hour 30 minutes, hands-on · Cook: 0 minutes · Total: 2 hours, then 2–4 days fermenting · Makes 1 jar (about 1.5 kg)

Ingredients

The cabbage and salt

  • 1 large head napa cabbage (about 1.5 kg), cut into bite squares (the fast build cuts small — more surface, faster ferment)
  • 1/2 cup coarse salt (about 100 g), dissolved in water to cover

The paste

  • 1/2 cup gochugaru (about 50 g)
  • 1.5 tbsp gochujang (about 30 g) — the modern marker
  • 3 tbsp fish sauce (about 45 ml)
  • 1 tbsp salted shrimp or extra fish sauce (about 15 g)
  • 8 cloves garlic (about 32 g), grated; a thumb of ginger (about 20 g), grated
  • 1/2 Asian pear (about 100 g), grated
  • 1 tsp sugar (about 4 g)
  • 100 g Korean radish, fine matchsticks; 4 scallions (about 60 g), cut

Instructions

  1. The paste whisked into a thick slurry — gochugaru, gochujang, fish sauce, shrimp, garlic, ginger, pear, sugar — then the radish and scallions folded through. Checkpoint — taste on a drained cabbage square: full kimchi register, deeper and rounder than the traditional paste — the gochujang's contribution, arriving as body rather than heat. Slightly too strong is correct; the ferment rounds it.
  2. The squeezed cabbage and paste massaged together by hand (gloves save your weekend) until every square wears the coat, packed into a clean jar — pressed hard, 3 cm of headspace, weight on top, loose lid. Checkpoint — brine rises over the cabbage within a day; top up with salted water if it lags. Under-liquid is the failure mode, same law as every ferment.
  3. **The faster clock:** room temperature 2–4 days at normal kitchen warmth (the traditional batch's window assumes a cooler kitchen), tasting from day two. Checkpoint — the readiness read: lively sourness, steady fizz at a stir, squares still crunchy at the bend. This build's smaller cuts and extra ferment surface run about a day faster than the quartered traditional jar — taste early, refrigerate at the snap-and-sour you want, and eat it through its shorter, punchier arc.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Refrigerated, 2–3 weeks on its faster arc — young and crunchy through week one, stew-grade sour by week three. The pasted squares into the jjigae pot (#9) at the elder stage is the arc's whole design. - Variations: revert to the traditional paste by dropping the gochujang (the honest A/B test); cucumber squares at a 1-day ferment; the radish-only fork runs the same week's jar.
