Doenjang Jjigae

Prep: 15 minutes · Cook: 20 minutes · Total: About 35 minutes · Serves 3–4 (one pot)

Ingredients

The stock

  • 800 ml water; a 10 cm piece of dried kelp (about 5 g), 6–8 dried anchovies (about 10 g), simmered 10 minutes and strained — or water, honestly, and a longer simmer

The paste base

  • 3 tbsp doenjang (about 60 g)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g) — the pot's only chile, and a modest one
  • 1 tsp gochujang (about 5 g), optional — the modern kitchen's bridge, and the reason this pot is here; the traditional pot skips it

The pot

  • 150 g firm tofu, cubed; 100 g zucchini, half-moons; 100 g potato or onion, sliced; 100 g kimchi (#2's jar), chopped if sour-aged
  • 2 scallions (about 30 g) and 2 green chilies (about 15 g), sliced; 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated; a splash of sesame oil to finish (about 5 ml)
  • A handful of enoki or shiitake mushrooms (about 60 g), optional

Instructions

  1. The dissolved paste, the gochugaru, and the optional gochujang into the simmering stock; the potato, onion, and zucchini in; 8 minutes at a steady bubble — the root vegetables need the head start. Checkpoint — the broth has gone from clear to cloudy-gold, and the smell is the ferment's deep, savory, slightly funky register — the smell this site's other pages orbit around. Taste: saltier than drinking broth, correct for a rice-adjacent stew.
  2. The tofu, kimchi, and mushrooms in, 5 more minutes — the tofu warms through and the kimchi's sour edge dissolves into the broth's depth. Checkpoint — the potato is tender at a poke, the tofu hot to its center, and the broth tastes of the two ferments and the sea's stock — the kimchi's age determining its sour presence (young jar: a bright note; elder jar: the pot's backbone).
  3. Garlic, scallions, chilies, and the sesame oil finish over the top, pot off the heat, straight to the table in its own vessel. Checkpoint — the eating test with rice: a spoon of broth, a piece of tofu, a bite of rice — the broth's funk and depth should make the rice disappear at a pace that embarrasses the cook's plans for leftovers. If the broth tastes flat, it wants salt's carrier — another half-spoon of doenjang dissolved in the ladle, not table salt.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 2 days and improves; the next day's reheated pot with fresh scallions over is the tradition's own day-after. (The dolsot's leftover components (#4) into this pot is the fridge's finest zero-waste move.) - Variations: the clam pot (a handful of the #SF15 clams in at step 2, their liquor the stock's upgrade); the beef-and-mushroom build; the full honesty pot with no gochujang at all — the tradition's own, and this site bows to it.
