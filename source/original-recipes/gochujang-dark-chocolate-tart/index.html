**Lane: fusion — the site's final argument, made in chocolate.** Mexican chocolate has known chile for five centuries; this tart is the Korean pantry's entry into that old pairing — a dark chocolate ganache, warm with gochujang's ferment-sweet-heat, in a cocoa-shortbread shell, with the salted sesame finish the tradition brings to its own sweets. The heat arrives after the chocolate, the ferment under the fruit of the cacao, and the first slice converts more skeptics than any savory record on this site. **Heat dial: 2 — dessert's ceiling, respectably held.**

The finish

- **The shell's dough handled like any shortbread: cold and brief.** Flour, cocoa, sugar, salt; the butter rubbed to a coarse meal; the yolk and just enough water to clump; pressed into the tart pan, chilled 30 minutes, then blind-baked at 175 °C (15 minutes with weights, 5 without). Cocoa dough tastes raw-flour bitter when underbaked — pull it at the smell of warm brownie, not by color alone. - **The gochujang whisked into the cream before it meets the chocolate** — the paste dissolves completely in warm cream and never in cooling chocolate; the order is the technique, and lumps of paste in set ganache are the recipe's only common failure.

Doneness & Quality Checkpoints

  • The shell: blind-baked at 175 °C to warm-brownie smell, cooled fully.
  • The cream: paste fully dissolved in the warm cream, never in the chocolate.
  • The ganache: three-addition emulsion, stirred to velvet, un-frothed.
  • The set: two hours, trembling-firm; the finish: sesame, salt, and the optional corner.

Gochujang Dark Chocolate Tart

Prep: 30 minutes, plus 2 hours chilling · Cook: 35 minutes · Total: About 3 hours 5 minutes · Makes one 23 cm tart (10–12 slices)

Ingredients

The shell

  • 150 g all-purpose flour; 30 g cocoa powder; 40 g sugar; a pinch of salt (about 2 g)
  • 90 g cold butter, cubed; 1 egg yolk (about 20 g); 1–2 tbsp ice water

The ganache

  • 250 g dark chocolate (64–70%), chopped
  • 200 ml heavy cream
  • 1.5 tbsp gochujang (about 30 g) — the full dose; a level tablespoon for dial 1.5
  • 2 tbsp honey (about 40 g)
  • 30 g butter, soft
  • A pinch of fine salt (about 2 g)

The finish

  • 1 tbsp black or toasted sesame seeds (about 8 g), flaky sea salt (about 3 g), and optional: a dusting of gochugaru (about 1 g) across the top, for the last visual tell

Instructions

  1. The cream warmed to a bare simmer with the gochujang and the honey, whisked as it warms until the paste fully dissolves and the cream turns a uniform pale-rose tan. Checkpoint — a cooled spoonful tastes of warm cream, honey, and the paste's ferment — gently savory-sweet, no chile burn yet. The cream carries the paste; the chocolate will carry everything.
  2. **The ganache built in three additions:** the chopped chocolate in a bowl, the hot gochujang cream poured over and waited on 60 seconds, then stirred from the center outward in slow circles — the emulsion building gradually, the gloss arriving in stages, never whisked to froth. The soft butter and salt stirred in at the gloss. Checkpoint — the ganache is a poured-velvet stream that coats a spatula and holds a light trail — glossy, dark, faintly rose-toned, smelling of chocolate with a warm savory undertone the nose files under "interesting." Frothed or split: it was whisked too hard or the butter was cold; the fix is gentle stirring over a warm water bath until it comes back.
  3. Poured into the cooled shell, the surface smoothed with a spatula's single pass (more passes leave streaks), and chilled 2 hours to a set that trembles slightly at the pan's tap. Checkpoint — the set's test at the pan's edge: the ganache is firm enough to cut clean and soft enough to melt at the mouth's temperature — the two-hour set, not the overnight brick.
  4. **The finish and the first slice:** sesame seeds and flaky salt scattered over the whole surface, the optional gochugaru's faint red dust across one quadrant (the last visual tell — and the table's game of spotting which slices came from the spicy corner). Checkpoint — the eating test, and this record's entire thesis: the first bite reads as an excellent dark chocolate tart — bitter-sweet, silky, the shortbread's cocoa snap. Then, four seconds in, the warmth arrives under the chocolate's finish — not burn, arrival — and the salt and sesame carry it out. The second bite waits for it. The third craves it. That progression is the fusion, complete.

Finish & Plate

Sliced thin — a 23 cm tart serves twelve honestly, because richness like this runs in narrow slices — with espresso or the sikhye register beside. Announce nothing, or announce everything; both servings of the tart have their own kind of party.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Keeps 3 days refrigerated (the ganache's flavor deepens on day two; the shell softens by day three — the second day is the peak). Freezes sliced, a month, thawed cold overnight. - Variations: the dial-1 family build; a sesame-praline layer under the ganache; the Mexican acknowledgment done right — a cinnamon thread in the cream beside the paste, the two chile-cultures' hand-shake in one pan.
