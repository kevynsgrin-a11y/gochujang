Yangnyeom Fried Chicken

Korean · Medium · Serves 4

Jump to Recipe Method Troubleshooting Storage
**Lane: modern — the double-fry and the toss, the paste's most famous delivery system.** Korean fried chicken's argument with the American original is thinness of crust and the sauce: a shattering, thin glass crust from a double fry, and — in the yangnyeom register — a gochujang glaze tossed at the last second, lacquering every crevice without ever sogging the crunch. This is the paste's biggest stage and the site's technique centerpiece: the brine, the starch dredge, the two-stage fry, and the fifteen-second toss. Get these four right and the chicken is the best thing at any table it joins. **Heat dial: 3, adjustable at the glaze.**

- **Brine the chicken 2 hours** — salt, sugar, garlic, and ginger doing quiet work while the oil sleeps. The brined bird fries juicier and seasons deeper than any post-fry sprinkle manages. - **Drain, dry, and dredge twice-thin:** the chicken patted bone-dry, then dredged in the starch mixture in a bag — shaken, not buried. Knock the excess off hard; the coating should be a whisper, a frost of starch, not a batter. Thin is the entire Korean crust argument. - **The glaze built before the fry** — oil, garlic, and gochujang fried together 45 seconds (the bloom, again, the lane's first law), then honey, soy, mirin, and gochugaru stirred in off the heat to a thick lacquer, parked beside the fry station with a wide bowl for the toss.

Yangnyeom Fried Chicken

Prep: 30 minutes, plus 2 hours brining · Cook: 30 minutes · Total: About 3 hours (brine ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The brine

  • 1.2 kg chicken wings and drumettes (or boneless thighs cut into strips)
  • 500 ml water, 2 tbsp salt (about 30 g), 2 tbsp sugar (about 25 g), 4 cloves garlic (about 16 g) smashed, a thumb of ginger (about 10 g) sliced — dissolved, then the chicken in, refrigerated 2 hours (up to 4)

The dredge

  • 120 g potato starch or cornstarch
  • 40 g all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp each salt and pepper (about 4 g)
  • A pinch of baking powder (about 2 g)

The fry

  • About 1.5 liters neutral oil, for the two-stage fry

The yangnyeom glaze

  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 2 tbsp honey or corn syrup (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp soy sauce (15 ml)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g) — dial control
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g); 3 cloves garlic (about 12 g), grated; 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g)
  • Optional: 1/2 tbsp ketchup (about 10 g), the old-school register

Instructions

  1. **First fry — cook through:** oil at 170 °C (a starch-dusted cube sizzles steadily, not violently), the chicken in uncrowded batches, 6–8 minutes to cooked-through and pale-gold. Drain on a rack. Checkpoint — the first-fry crust is dry, matte, and the meat cooked (a drumette's joint end shows clear juices); this stage sets the crust's architecture and renders the skin's water out from under it.
  2. **Second fry — the shatter:** oil up to 190 °C, the same pieces returned for 90 seconds to 2 minutes, until the crust goes deep gold and audibly crackles when two pieces touch. Checkpoint — the crust's test: tap a piece against the rack's edge — it should sound and crack like thin glass. The double fry's physics: the first stage's steam-puffed starch layer sets, and the second's higher heat blasts the residual moisture out of it, leaving the honeycomb of crackle. This is not repetition; it is construction.
  3. **The fifteen-second toss — the sauce's law.** The hot chicken into the wide bowl, the warm glaze over, and tossed with two spatulas for ten to fifteen seconds — every surface lacquered, the bowl scraped, service immediate. Checkpoint — the eating test, thirty seconds after the toss: the crust still shatters under the glaze (the toss's brevity is what preserves it — the glaze coats the outside of the crunch, it does not get time to soak in), the sauce's sweet-heat arriving with the crunch, the brined meat juicy underneath. A yangnyeom that has sat five minutes is still good; a yangnyeom eaten in the first minute is the dish this record exists for.

Finish & Plate

Piled high and lacquered on a platter with pickled radish cubes (the traditional coolant — sweet-sour against the glaze's heat) and cold beer or the sikhye register beside. Napkins beyond reason; the dish demands them.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The brined, dredged chicken holds 1 day refrigerated before frying. The fried-undressed pieces re-crisp at 190 °C for 60 seconds the next day — then toss fresh. Dressed yangnyeom keeps nothing, by design. - Variations: the undressed half with the cho-gochujang dip (#13's second bowl); soy-garlic (the chicken-house's other classic — the glaze's gochujang replaced by soy, butter, and a mountain of sliced garlic); the boneless-strip build for the lunchbox and the buns.
