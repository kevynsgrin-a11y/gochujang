Gochujang-Miso Black Cod

Prep: 10 minutes, plus 24 hours curing · Cook: 12 minutes · Total: 10 minutes on the day (cure ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The fish

  • 4 black cod (sablefish) fillets (about 700 g), pin bones pulled

The cure

  • 3 tbsp white miso (about 60 g)
  • 2 tbsp gochujang (about 40 g)
  • 2 tbsp mirin (30 ml)
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil (15 ml)

The finish and table

  • Neutral oil for the pan (about 10 ml)
  • Toasted sesame seeds (about 4 g), sliced scallion (about 10 g)
  • Pickled cucumber or perilla, rice (about 400 g cooked)

Instructions

  1. A heavy pan at medium with the film of oil; fillets in presentation-side-down, 4 minutes to deep tea-brown caramel — the sugars' clock, watched, not set-and-forgotten. Checkpoint — the face lifts clean and reads dark caramel, not black. Smoking hard: the heat is a notch past the sugars' tolerance — lower and give the pan a minute.
  2. Flip, 3 minutes more on the second side (a fillet's thickness giving 7 minutes total on the pan), and pull at the flake stage — the flesh parts along the grain at a fork's weight, translucent-glossy at the very center. Checkpoint — the test flake: lacquered outside, butter-silk inside, the two ferments' depth all the way through — miso's sweetness and the paste's warm hum arriving one after the other. That sequence is the fusion's signature.
  3. Sesame and scallion over, plated on rice with the sharp pickle beside. Checkpoint — the eating test with the pickle in the same bite-set: rich, savory-sweet, a late warmth, then the acid's reset. If the heat reads before the miso, the gochujang ran heavy — a tablespoon less next cure.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- Curing fillets hold 36 hours (the clock above); cooked, 2 days — cold flakes over rice with sesame oil is the day-after luxury. - Variations: salmon at a 12-hour cure; the braise-glaze (#1) thinned as a warm sauce over; a drop of honey in the final minute under the broiler for the lacquered ceiling.
