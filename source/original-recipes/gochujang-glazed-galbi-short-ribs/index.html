Gochujang-Glazed Galbi Short Ribs

Prep: 25 minutes, plus 12–24 hours marinating · Cook: 3 hours · Total: About 3 hours 30 minutes (marinate ahead) · Serves 4

Ingredients

The ribs

  • 1.8 kg English-cut bone-in beef short ribs (thick, 5–7 cm blocks)

The braise

  • 1 tbsp neutral oil (about 12 g)
  • 1 large onion (about 200 g), thick-sliced; 6 cloves garlic (about 24 g), smashed; a 5 cm piece of ginger (about 25 g), sliced
  • 4 tbsp gochujang (about 80 g)
  • 60 ml soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp honey (about 40 g)
  • 1 tbsp mirin (15 ml)
  • 1 Asian pear (about 300 g), quartered (the braise's tenderizer and body — strained out later)
  • 700 ml water or beef stock
  • 2 dried chilies or 1 tsp gochugaru (about 3 g), for dial 3

The glaze (built from the strained braise)

  • The braising liquid, reduced
  • 1 tbsp honey (about 20 g), extra
  • A squeeze of lemon or splash of rice vinegar (about 10 ml)

The table

  • Steamed rice (about 600 g cooked), scallion threads, sesame seeds (about 8 g), quick-pickled radish or cucumber

Instructions

  1. **The braise: seared ribs, aromatics, and everything liquid into the pot.** The gochujang fried in the fond for 30 seconds before the liquids join (the bloom — the spine's first law), then soy, honey, mirin, pear, water, and the chilies over the ribs. Lid ajar, the barest simmer (a bubble every few seconds — 95 °C at the pot's edge, never a boil), 2.5–3 hours, turning the ribs once at the halfway mark. Checkpoint — the fork test at 2.5 hours: the meat yields to a fork's twist with no fight, the bone slides when pulled, and the braise smells deep-sweet-savory. Not there: 30 more minutes and check again. A boiled-tight braise means the heat ran — the low simmer is the recipe.
  2. **Strain and reduce — the glaze is the braise, concentrated.** Ribs out to a tray; the liquid through a fine strainer (the pear and aromatics have given everything — discard them), the fat spooned off the top, and the remaining liquid reduced at a hard simmer, 15–25 minutes, to a sauce that coats a spoon and holds a dragged trail. Taste: it should be intense — salty-sweet-fermented, a shade too strong alone — and then the extra honey and the lemon go in to round it. Checkpoint — the trail-holds test on the spoon, and the glaze's color gone from broth-brown to lacquer-red. This is the glaze the ribs are about to wear.
  3. **The broiler lacquer.** Ribs on a foil-lined tray, glaze brushed thick on all meat faces, and under a hot broiler (or a 230 °C oven's top rack) 3–4 minutes a side, watching through the door — the sugars go from lacquered to burnt in one careless minute. Two coats, two broiler passes, for the full build. Checkpoint — the surface: glossy, blistered at the edges, charred only at the corners — and a test pull at a bone-end strip should taste like the whole argument: the braise's depth, the glaze's sweet-heat edge, the char's smoke on top.
  4. **Serve on rice with the remaining glaze at the table** — there should be a small bowl of it, because the ribs' last act is dragging rice through the leftover lacquer. Checkpoint — the eating test: a bite of the rib's thick center with rice and the pickled crunch beside it — the meat should barely hold together, the glaze should announce the paste's ferment under its sweetness, and the pickle should reset the whole board. That triangle is the dish.

Finish & Plate

One or two rib blocks per person over rice, glaze brushed at the pass and the bowl of extra glaze at the table's center. Scallion threads and sesame over the ribs, the pickle plate beside, and spoons — this is a dish eaten with a spoon in the other hand.

Storage, Make-Ahead & Variations

- The braise keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves; glaze-and-broil at the serving pass. The full glaze-braised-and-lacquered leftovers reheat under the broiler in 4 minutes — arguably the best office lunch in this entire corpus. - Variations: the grill-site's thin flanken cousin (kbbqguide#M04) for the fast fork; a spoon of the glaze thinned over the black cod (#11); dial up with fresh chili minced into the final coat.
